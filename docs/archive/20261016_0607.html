The Physics Is the Network: Fast, Training-Free Neural Emulation
Authors: Luka Todorovic, Kaushal Gianchandani, Sai Ravela
First: 2026-10-14T17:24:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:24:48+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Neural emulation need not require learning a substitute for known physics. We investigate PolyNets that couple a continuous-time neural representation of polynomial dynamics with a simple recurrent network for discrete-time integration. When the governing coefficients are known, the resulting tensor graph requires no training and preserves the prescribed numerical scheme while enabling efficient hardware execution and parallel simulation. Using the one-dimensional Korteweg-de Vries equation, we compare PolyNet with CPU and GPU finite-difference Runge-Kutta solvers and three trained neural models: a state-map emulator, a physics-informed predictor, and a learned-tendency integrator. Analytical soliton solutions provide a reference for amplitude and phase errors across grid resolutions, 10^6 dispersively scaled time steps, ensembles of up to 10^4 trajectories, and varying initial amplitudes. PolyNet achieves accuracy comparable to the numerical solvers, including at amplitudes outside the learned models' training conditions. It combines this fidelity with high GPU throughput, approximately constant over the tested resolution range, and favorable ensemble scaling.

Decoupled Marginal Sharpening for Training-Free Inference-Time Scaling
Authors: Weixin Wang, Wei Deng, Anderson Schneider, Yuriy Nevmyvaka, Pan Xu, Andrew Bennett
First: 2026-10-14T16:55:22+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T16:55:22+00:00
Comments: 17 pages, 3 figures, 6 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study how to improve LLM reasoning at inference time without updating model weights or using external reward models. Recent reward-free power-sampling methods define the sequence-level power target $π_α(y\mid x)\propto p_0(y\mid x)^α$, where $α>1$, which increases the relative probability of completions that are already likely under the base model. Existing methods use Metropolis--Hastings or Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) to approximately sample from this target. However, two challenges arise at different levels. At the target level, full-sequence sharpening can concentrate probability on a few exact reasoning--answer trajectories, suppressing alternative paths that support the same answer. At the sampler level, finite-particle SMC can suffer from path collapse when resampling repeatedly duplicates a few high-weight prefixes. To address these challenges, we propose Decoupled Marginal Sharpening (DMS), which separately controls how strongly the model favors particular reasoning paths and how strongly it favors final answers after aggregating support across those paths. At the target level, DMS first samples from a sharpened reasoning distribution, then marginalizes the model's answer probabilities over the resulting weighted reasoning population and sharpens the induced answer marginal. Separate exponents allow mild reasoning sharpening to encourage exploration of diverse paths, followed by stronger answer-marginal sharpening to concentrate probability on answers with greater aggregate support across those paths. At the sampler level, we develop Fully Adapted Annealed SMC, which combines an annealed sequence of power targets with locally fully adapted proposals and predictive ancestor resampling. Experiments on MATH500, GPQA-Diamond, HumanEval, and AIME24/25 across three base models show that DMS achieves the highest mean pass@1 among the compared methods in all tasks.

OmniSelect: Dynamic Modality-Aware Token Compression for Efficient Omni-modal Large Language Models

Abstract
Omnimodal large language models (OmniLLMs) have recently gained increasing attention for unified audio-video understanding. However, processing long multimodal token sequences introduces substantial computational overhead, making efficient token compression crucial. Existing methods typically rely on fixed, modality-specific guidance, which fails to account for the varying importance of modalities across different queries. To address this limitation, we propose $\textbf{OmniSelect}$, a training-free, modality-adaptive token pruning framework that dynamically selects appropriate compression strategies for multimodal inputs. Specifically, we leverage a lightweight AudioCLIP model to estimate cross-modal relevance and categorize each input into three pruning regimes: Audio-Centric, Video-Centric, and Uniform pruning. Based on these relevance scores, OmniSelect further performs fine-grained token pruning within each temporal group, adaptively allocating pruning ratios to preserve informative tokens across modalities. By explicitly modeling modality preference and enabling dynamic strategy selection, OmniSelect effectively avoids the pitfalls of one-size-fits-all compression. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method achieves efficient multimodal token reduction while maintaining strong performance, without requiring any additional training.

Equal CER, Unequal Damage: Severity-Weighted Character Error Rate for OCR

Abstract
Character Error Rate (CER), the standard metric for OCR and vision-language models (VLMs), charges a typo as much as a wrong name. We introduce semCER, which keeps CER's alignment and denominator and charges each grapheme edit a severity from an LLM judge, with no word segmentation. Normalized CER keeps the score of a whole transcription, where it tracks readers as well as semCER or better, and semCER breaks that score down into named edits. The charges track readers' ratings edit by edit in English, Vietnamese, and Chinese and put the edits readers rate most severe near the top of a sorted list. On pairs drawn where CER and semCER disagreed, readers side with semCER more often than with normalized CER in English and Chinese, and equally often in Vietnamese. Across 16 languages, the judge charges a wrong diacritic or tone mark almost nothing in Spanish but nearly a wrong letter's severity in Telugu. Where we can count real words, a general VLM's wrong letter forms one far above chance while a classical pipeline's stays near it. We release the 61 million judged edits, the aggregated reader ratings, and the distilled scorer at https://github.com/nguyenx2t/semcer.

The COUNT: Counting N-grams Unlocks Scaling on Host Memory and Training-Free Adaptation
Authors: Ian Magnusson, Noah A. Smith, Pang Wei Koh
First: 2026-10-14T16:38:19+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T16:38:19+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The scaling relationship of compute and performance for language models (LMs) has been extensively studied; more recently, models are being designed to scale memory use, as well. In particular, adding embeddings for n-grams scales memory separately from compute use, because each pass only processes individual embedding rows, which are much smaller than the storage used for the whole embedding table. However, existing methods train embeddings from scratch, which needs expensive GPU memory for fast reading and writing. Meanwhile, the ordinary memory of the host system is cheap and underutilized by these training methods. To scale on host memory, we introduce COUNT (Counting Offline Unlocks N-gram Transformers), which builds n-gram embeddings without adding learned parameters by storing precomputed n-gram counts on host. We use the corpus frequencies to weight together the LM's own unembeddings of the next tokens most often following the current n-gram history. At a fixed compute budget, COUNT can be added as an additional input embedding for pretraining 1B-parameter LMs to improve validation loss as much as an LM with 57% more compute. If GPU memory is limited, using COUNT to let backbone parameters fill GPU memory gives better performance than splitting memory with additional learned n-gram embeddings. Beyond efficiency, COUNT allows switching in a different table of n-gram counts from a domain-specific corpus at inference time to adapt to a new task without any training.

Reward Design for Physical Reasoning in Vision-Language Models
Authors: Derek Lilienthal, Manisha Mukherjee, Sameera Horawalavithana

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Physical reasoning over images requires integrating perception, domain knowledge, and multi-step symbolic inference, yet Vision-Language Models (VLMs) fall far short of human performance on physics benchmarks. Reinforcement learning can improve reasoning via reward signals, but it remains unclear whether these signals must come from outside the model or can be derived from the model itself. We compare external rewards, which rely on an outside verifier (rule-based format and answer-correctness checks, or an LLM-judge rubric), with an internal attention-based reward computed from the model's own attention over image regions, requiring no labels or judge. We find that external rewards drive most of the accuracy gains, while preserving more coherent reasoning when rewarded for reasoning tokens. The internal attention-based reward, provides an annotation-free grounding signal that improves spatial-relation reasoning and is complementary to external rewards: adding it to format and accuracy rewards, at no additional annotation cost, improves average accuracy on Molmo~2 while generalizing to out-of-distribution tasks. Our results suggest that internal attention rewards can supplement external supervision and lead to improved physical reasoning.

FOLD: Position-Free Far Memory with Local Order for Long-Context Speculative Decoding
Authors: Shuxuan Li, Jialiang Kang, Yanghao Zhou, Ziye Zhu, Xiao Yang
First: 2026-10-14T15:55:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T15:55:10+00:00

Abstract
Speculative decoding accelerates LLM generation through lightweight drafting and parallel verification. It is particularly promising for long inputs, where KV-cache access costs can be amortized over multiple accepted tokens. Yet most drafters are trained on short sequences and degrade on long inputs, while long-context training is costly. Existing training-free approaches restrict the context window, rescale positions, or augment recent context with retrieved distant entries. Drafters, however, read target hidden states that already encode contextual and positional information, and their lightweight architecture makes full-history attention affordable. We propose FOLD, a training-free method that preserves exact local positions and treats all distant entries as far memory at a shared position. To counter attention dilution as the memory grows, we sharpen attention within it to emphasize relevant entries while separately controlling its total weight relative to the local window. Across six target-drafter pairs and diverse benchmarks of up to 256K tokens, FOLD predominantly delivers the highest speedup, with the best case more than doubling the speedup over autoregressive decoding, from 2.08$\times$ to 4.22$\times$.

Abstract

Abstract
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have made substantial progress in spatial reasoning, and self-evolving agents further extend these capabilities to embodied environments by learning from interactions. However, current self-evolution remains particularly challenging in embodied spatial tasks, complex and multi-step decisions from partial visual evidence making it difficult to determine why an execution fails. The failure may stem from misperception, insufficient exploration, or incorrect planning, yet execution feedback alone rarely reveals which component is responsible. As a result, simply accumulating interaction experience can reinforce ineffective strategies. To address this, we propose HiMeco, a brain-inspired self-evolving agent for embodied spatial intelligence, inspired by metacognitive monitoring and hippocampal memory consolidation. Through Hippocampal-Metacognitive Reflection, HiMeco diagnoses failures in perception-exploration-planning (PEP), then compares execution attempts to derive reusable strategies and guidance for avoiding recurring errors. It also abstracts interaction programs into parameterized skills, using evidence contracts and execution-based validation to determine their retention and reuse. These strategies and skills guide subsequent interactions, with memory updated only between rounds and model parameters kept frozen. HiMeco achieves state-of-the-art performance on ESI-Bench, and it also improves accuracy on EmbSpatial-Bench by 3.94 percentage points without evolution.

Abstract
Vision-language models have shown strong performance, but they often generalize poorly to specialized domains. While semi-supervised vision-language learning mitigates this limitation by leveraging a small set of labeled image-text pairs together with abundant unlabeled images, existing methods remain fundamentally pairwise and fail to model the global structure of multimodal representation manifolds. Most existing topology-based alignment methods rely on persistence diagram matching, which neither guarantees geometric alignment nor uses the image-text pairing information central to vision-language learning. We propose Topology-Aware Representation Alignment for Semi-Supervised Vision-Language Learning (ToMA), a framework that uses persistent homology to decompose pairwise relations into $H_0$-death and $H_1$-birth relations and aligns them across modalities through available cross-modal correspondences. This lightweight decomposition captures both connectivity and cycle-closing structure without constructing 2-simplices. Experiments show clear improvements on remote sensing, while fashion results reveal strong gains in classification and particularly strong retrieval improvements for difficult queries. Additional analysis shows that ToMA provides more consistent gains than alternative topology-based objectives and that lightweight $H_1$-birth edges provide useful higher-order structural signals.

Seeing at the Edge: Energy Characterization of Vision-Language Model Inference on Edge Platforms
Authors: Junlin Liao, Harshit Kondle, Di Liu, Zonghua Gu
First: 2026-10-14T12:55:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T12:55:12+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) are moving onto edge devices, but little is known about how much energy they draw on single-board computers with NPU or GPU accelerators, or where that energy goes. We measure total-system energy, latency, and thermal behavior of Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct and its text-only sibling on a Raspberry Pi 5 with a Hailo-10H NPU and on a Jetson Orin Nano Super, on both CPU and accelerator, and separate the fixed energy of each request from the energy that grows with output length. Because the accelerators use different quantization and serving software, we compare deployment stacks rather than silicon. Three findings stand out. First, NPU offloading makes the Pi a much more efficient VLM platform: it cuts energy per generated token by about two-thirds, leaves the host CPU largely free, and avoids the thermal throttling that the CPU backend reaches in sustained use. Second, an image adds both fixed and per-token energy, but at typical response lengths generating the answer costs far more than processing the image. Third, the Jetson GPU favors short responses through its lower fixed cost per request, whereas the Pi NPU favors longer ones through its lower cost per output token; their energy curves cross near 40 output tokens.

First: 2026-10-14T11:56:53+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T11:56:53+00:00

Abstract
The rapid advancement of large vision language models (LVLMs) has made geolocation inference from images highly accurate and accessible. This poses serious privacy risks: a passive adversary with black-box LVLM access can infer a user's location from a single photo with no metadata. Existing protection methods rely almost exclusively on adversarial perturbation, which introduces perceptible visual artifacts and utility degradation. We identify the visual-geolocation asymmetry: geolocation-decisive clues, such as street signs, tend to be visually peripheral but dominate location inference. Exploiting this asymmetry, we propose GeoCloak, an agentic framework for semantics-preserving geolocation privacy protection. It acts as a clue-aware planner that analyzes each image to identify geolocation-relevant clues, dispatches targeted semantic edits to directly suppress peripheral clues, and autonomously configures adversarial perturbation to further suppress residual geolocation features, all under iterative utility-aware feedback. We evaluate GeoCloak on four diverse datasets spanning 108 countries and 576 cities against five LVLMs and three inference methods. GeoCloak reduces overall city-level accuracy from 41.2% to 9.9%, approximately half of the best-performing baseline, while achieving substantially superior utility preservation both qualitatively and quantitatively. We hope this work takes a meaningful step toward practical, semantics-preserving geolocation privacy protection, giving users greater control over the location information inadvertently embedded in their shared images.

Abstract
Diffusion Language Models (dLLMs) have garnered significant attention for their potential in highly parallel processing. The parallel capabilities of existing dLLMs stem from the assumption of conditional independence at high confidence levels, which ensures negligible discrepancy between the marginal and joint distributions. However, the stringent confidence thresholds required to preserve accuracy severely constrain the scalability of parallelism. Through systematic token-level statistical analysis, we reveal that a substantial proportion of tokens converge to their correct predictions early in the denoising process yet fail to reach standard confidence thresholds, confirming that current confidence-based criteria are overly conservative. In response, we introduce LEAP (Lookahead Early-Convergence Token Detection for Accelerated Parallel Decoding). LEAP is a training-free, plug-and-play method that leverages future context filtering and multi-sequence superposition to detect early-converging tokens. By validating the alignment between early convergence and correctness, we enable reliable early decoding of these tokens. Benchmarking across diverse domains demonstrates that LEAP significantly lowers inference latency and decoding steps. Compared to confidence-based decoding, the average number of denoising steps is reduced by about 30%. On the GSM8K dataset, combining LEAP with dParallel accelerates decoding to 7.2 tokens per step while preserving model precision. LEAP effectively breaks the reliance on high-confidence priors, offering a novel paradigm for parallel decoding.

Abstract

Abstract
LiDAR scene flow is the task of estimating point-wise motion from sequential point clouds. It is essential for autonomous driving, as it provides critical information about moving objects and their trajectories. In recent years, unsupervised methods have gained significant traction by eliminating the need for expensive point-wise annotations. However, despite incremental performance improvements, these methods uniformly rely on a single paradigm: training feed-forward networks through optimizing a loss function based on the Chamfer distance, a framework that inherently limits their progression. This paper presents CorrelationFlow, a new paradigm that reformulates LiDAR scene flow as a correlation maximization problem between Bird's-Eye View (BEV) images. Instead of using learnable parameters, our approach employs a chain of image processing operations to solve this problem. The learning-free nature of CorrelationFlow enables strong generalization across diverse domains. This capability is demonstrated by the second-place ranking in the unsupervised track of the Argoverse 2 Scene Flow Challenge at CVPR 2026, which featured a test set spanning five datasets that cover varied vehicle platforms and environments. Our results show that this classical, model-based approach can match or even surpass the performance of learning-based methods, thereby opening a new direction for unsupervised LiDAR scene flow research. Abstract

PhyMetric: Diagnosing Physical Plausibility in Text-to-Video Generation via Scene-Level QA
Authors: Siwei Meng, Xuerou Cai, Rui Hu, Ping Liu

Abstract
Text-to-video (T2V) generation models can produce visually realistic videos that nevertheless violate the physical consequences implied by the prompt and scene. Existing benchmarks largely rely on holistic plausibility scores or broad quality dimensions, leaving it unclear which physical behavior is being tested and why a model fails. We introduce PhyMetric, a scene-level diagnostic benchmark that recasts physical plausibility evaluation as verifiable, scene-grounded question answering. PhyMetric contains 1{,}115 scenes spanning 50 physical categories across six domains, paired with 8{,}920 answer-balanced binary questions over 12 evaluation dimensions. Each scene is associated with eight targeted yes/no questions and a structured physical prior specifying the governing law, expected temporal sequence, observable signatures, and common failure modes. At evaluation time, a vision-language model (VLM) judge answers each question from question-aware sampled frames conditioned on the prior, and correctness is hierarchically aggregated into scene, dimension, physical category, and model scores. Experiments on four open-source T2V models and three VLM judges show that scene-specific binary QA is a critical component of the protocol: when each targeted question is replaced by a single generic plausibility query, evaluator accuracy drops to the chance level. Structured physical priors offer a complementary signal that improves human alignment of the resulting scores, with effects that vary across VLM judges of different capacity. These findings establish PhyMetric as a benchmark that turns physical plausibility evaluation from opaque holistic scoring into interpretable, scene-level inspection. Our project page is available at: https://mengsiwei.github.io/PhyMetric/.

A strong LLM strategic agent should reason prospectively over uncertain futures, adapt its strategy to opponents' behavioral tendencies, and continuously recalibrate its decision process from interaction experience. However, incorporating these sources in free-form reasoning could lead to unsupported strategic assumptions, inconsistent opponent estimates, and harmful interference from irrelevant historical interactions. To address these issues, we propose SAGE, a training-free inference-time framework that structures LLM strategic reasoning around three coordinated operations: anchor, adapt, and recalibrate. SAGE first anchors reasoning to an equilibrium policy that provides a strategically valid prior. It then conditions deviations from this anchor on a soft belief over opponent behavioral tendencies, enabling opponent-specific exploitation. Finally, SAGE distills strategically related interactions into counterfactual hypotheses about previously missing considerations, allowing past experience to recalibrate the model's reasoning. We evaluate SAGE on three repeated imperfect-information games: Leduc Hold'em, Liar's Dice, and Goofspiel, against various opponent types in each game. Compared with reasoning-intensive LLM agents, including Suspicion-Agent, ReTA, Agent-Pro, EMO, and Hypothetical Minds, SAGE achieves up to a 127.6% payoff improvement in Liar's Dice while reducing input and output token usage by up to 80% and 90%, respectively. In direct match-up play, it attains non-negative mean payoff against 5/10, 8/10, and 8/10 evaluated opponents in Leduc Hold'em, Liar's Dice, and Goofspiel, respectively, while using relatively fewer tokens. Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) with billions of parameters are prime targets for network pruning, which removes some model weights without hurting performance. Prior approaches such as magnitude pruning, SparseGPT, and Wanda either concentrated solely on weights or integrated weights with activations for sparsity. Abstract
Discriminative models are trained with task-specific objectives such as classification, detection, or representation learning, yet their practical utility depends on generalizing beyond the training task to unseen samples from the same domain. Abstract
Video large language models (Video LLMs) often generate responses unsupported by the input video. Contrastive decoding (CD) can mitigate these hallucinations, but its effectiveness depends on how the negative branch weakens visual evidence. Existing methods rely on predefined perturbations or masking guided by question reconstruction, offering limited control over the model's visual reliance during answer generation. We introduce Attention-guided Representation Perturbation for Contrastive Decoding (ARP-CD), a training-free framework for constructing contrastive negatives directly in representation space. ARP-CD uses the attention mass assigned to visual tokens at the answer-prediction position to guide bounded gradient updates of the language model's input visual hidden states. These updates reduce visual attention and produce a negative input, whose predictions are contrasted with those of the original branch throughout generation. Experiments across four Video LLMs and four benchmarks show that ARP-CD outperforms representative training-free baselines in most model--benchmark settings, reducing hallucinations while improving general video understanding. Further analyses show that attention-guided perturbation directions improve negative quality and that the resulting negatives provide effective contrastive references for correcting the original predictions.

Abstract
