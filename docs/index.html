Which Tokens to Merge? Diffusion Dynamics for Efficient Image Generation
Authors: SeungJu Cha, Ye-Chan Kim, HyunGee Kim, Sungho Koh, Dong-Jin Kim
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-15T17:59:35+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:59:35+00:00
Comments: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Code: https://github.com/SeungJuCha/DaTMe
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Token merging has emerged as an effective training-free strategy for accelerating diffusion models by reducing redundant token computation while minimizing generation degradation. A key challenge is determining which tokens can be safely merged and which should be preserved for subsequent computation. Existing method addresses this by preserving prompt-aligned foreground regions, thereby protecting semantically relevant structures. However, this text-relevance criterion overlooks background objects, local details that are not explicitly mentioned in the prompt, leading to degraded structural coherence. In this work, we propose Dynamics-aware Token Merging (DaTMe), a training-free framework that grounds token merging in the underlying diffusion dynamics. Our key insight is that token importance should reflect whether a token remains unresolved during denoising and whether it carries structural information. To this end, DaTMe constructs a dynamics- aware importance map from the Tweedie denoised estimate. The map combines a temporal signal, measuring inter-step changes in the predicted clean image, with a structural signal, capturing local high-frequency content such as boundaries and fine details. Using this map, DaTMe adaptively assigns tokens into merging roles under a given compression ratio: important tokens are preserved as anchors, while tokens that are both temporally stabilized and structurally homogeneous are selected as safe merge candidates. Experiments on PixArt-a and FLUX demonstrate that DaTMe maintains visual fidelity across the entire image, highlighting diffusion dynamics as an effective token-level merging decision in efficient generation. Our code is available at https://github.com/SeungJuCha/DaTMe.

SoftCache: Adaptive Step Size Caching for Diffusion Transformers
Authors: Shimon Malnick, Nadav Orenstein, Ohad Fried, Shai Avidan
First: 2026-10-15T17:59:34+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:59:34+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://malnick.net/softcache
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Diffusion and flow models generate a sample over iterative steps, each a full pass through a large network, and that pass dominates generation cost. Training-free caching accelerates them by reusing activations from recent evaluations instead of recomputing them. Dynamic caching methods decide when to refresh by accumulating a cheap probe of the network input into a staleness signal and spending a fresh evaluation once it crosses a threshold. They perform hard caching: the signal is a binary switch between refresh and reuse, and every step, fresh or cached, advances by the prescribed step size. We observe that the signal carries much more than this switch. How far it sits below the threshold, its headroom, predicts the error induced by reuse. We propose SoftCache, which performs soft caching: instead of a binary switch, the headroom sets how far each cached step goes. Where the headroom is large, the sampler jumps past several scheduled nodes, and as a refresh nears, its steps shrink back toward the original step. These adaptive jumps may land between the scheduled nodes, taking the sampler off its fixed grid. SoftCache requires no training and adds no per-backbone settings: on FLUX for images and on HunyuanVideo and Wan2.1 for video, at speedups from $3.5\times$ to $6.9\times$, it is ahead of every baseline on all four fidelity metrics.

Perceive, Predict, and Plan: a Unified World Action Model for Egocentric Dexterous Manipulation
Authors: Zirui Wu, Chen Guo, Tianjian Jiang, Zeren Jiang, Zeming Li, Lingchen Yang, Longteng Duan, Ying Xue, Handi Yin, Shenyuan Gao, Martin R. Oswald, Jun Zhang, Ping Tan, Jie Song

Abstract
We introduce P$^{3}$-WAM, a unified world action model for egocentric dexterous manipulation that learns 3D perception, action-conditioned prediction, and goal-directed planning through three complementary modes: Perceive, which reconstructs articulated hand motion and camera trajectories from video; Predict, which generates visual futures under prescribed actions; and Plan, which jointly generates action trajectories and their anticipated visual consequences conditioned on an initial image, a language instruction, and an optional visual goal. Our model integrates a compact, structured 3D action representation into a pretrained video diffusion transformer through a mixture-of-transformers architecture with shared base weights, modality-specific adaptation, and bidirectional visual-action attention. Randomized modality visibility lets video and 3D actions serve as either conditioning context or prediction targets across the three modes. Experiments show that unified training improves all three capabilities over single-mode training, including gains in planning quality under a vision-language model evaluation. Through unified training, our model outperforms specialized baselines on 3D hand reconstruction and action-conditioned video prediction across four datasets, while producing goal-directed manipulation videos with stronger instruction adherence and action progression than video-generation baselines. DK-VLM: Geometry as Position for Spatial Reasoning in Vision-Language Models
Authors: Han Su, Meng Tian, Tianyu Huang, Mingyu Gao, Lennox Wei, Dongxu Li, Jianhua Han, Hang Xu, Xiaohe Wu, Wangmeng Zuo
First: 2026-10-15T17:58:15+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:58:15+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://sumuru789.github.io/DK-VLM-website/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Vision-language models still struggle to understand 3D spatial relations across views and update spatial judgments as the observer moves. A common approach fuses 3D reconstruction features into visual tokens, but aligning these features with pretrained semantic representations remains challenging under limited spatial supervision. We propose DK-VLM with Dual-Kernel Attention, which computes attention from both a visual token's input position and its geometric position in the scene. A semantic kernel retains the pretrained model's native attention and positional encoding, while a separate geometric kernel uses estimated 3D points and camera poses to directly modulate attention through point displacements and relative view transformations. Then, a learned gate fuses their outputs. Geometry thus enters attention as position rather than as fused reconstruction features. We further introduce Reference-Frame Composition (RC) supervision to train the model to update its reference frame across successive judgments. It provides stepwise turn and heading targets derived from verified single-step annotations. Among the compared methods, DK-VLM-8B achieves the highest average score across six spatial benchmarks and the highest overall scores on VSI-Bench (74.33) and ReVSI-32 (58.28). RC supervision further improves multi-step spatial reasoning, while the full model preserves general video understanding. Project page: https://sumuru789.github.io/DK-VLM-website/.

Speculative Forcing for Efficient Autoregressive Video Generation
Authors: Hyunwook Choi, Seungryong Kim, Dongyoon Han, Sangdoo Yun
First: 2026-10-15T17:57:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:57:01+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Few-step autoregressive video generators enable streaming generation, yet they run every token through the final denoising step, regardless of how much refinement it needs. We find this uniform computation is often unnecessary: many tokens already closely match their eventual full-step outputs in the initial steps, while others require refinement until the end. We introduce Speculative Forcing, a framework that treats intermediate clean estimates as drafts and predicts when individual tokens can be safely accepted early. Once accepted, a token is frozen as its final output and undergoes no further denoising, while its cached keys and values remain available as context for the remaining tokens. We explore two alternative verifier designs: a training-free one based on similarity to clean content and stability across steps, and a learned one that reads the frozen generator's intermediate features to estimate each token's remaining gap to its full-step output. Across short-video, long-video, and world generation, Speculative Forcing preserves the quality of full-step sampling while substantially reducing computation. On LongLive, it matches the four-step generator with ~1.7 denoising steps per token, running over 1.5x faster at 41.7 and 37.8 fps. On Lingbot-World 2.0, where camera motion continually changes the visible content, it maintains quality with ~2.5 denoising steps per token and runs 1.35x faster at 24.5 fps.

EmbodiedMidtrain: Bridging the Gap between Vision-Language Models and Vision-Language-Action Models via Mid-training
Authors: Yiyang Du, Zhanqiu Guo, Haochen Zhang, Annabella Macaluso, Xin Ye, Liu Ren, Yonatan Bisk, Chenyan Xiong
First: 2026-04-21T21:40:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:55:44+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs) inherit their visual and linguistic capabilities from Vision-Language Models (VLMs), yet most VLAs are built from off-the-shelf VLMs that are not adapted to the embodied domain, limiting their downstream performance. In this work, we propose EmbodiedMidtrain to bridge the gap between VLMs and VLAs. We first characterize the data distribution gap between them, showing that VLA data occupy compact regions that are largely separated from the broader VLM distribution, while the degree of alignment varies substantially both across and within VLM data sources. Then, we build a mid-training data engine that leverages a lightweight learnable proximity estimator to select the most VLA-aligned candidates from a large VLM pool, and produce EmbodiedMidtrain-1M, a VLM dataset that we further use to mid-train the VLM before downstream VLA fine-tuning. Experiments on both simulated robot manipulation benchmarks and real-world tasks show consistent VLA performance gains over off-the-shelf checkpoints across VLM backbones and VLA action heads. These gains extend to downstream tasks not used to guide data selection. Further analysis reveals that mid-training provides a stronger initialization for VLA fine-tuning, with gains emerging from the earliest steps and widening throughout training. Moreover, the data engine captures both dataset-level and sample-level alignment signals, favoring spatial reasoning over text-centric tasks while preserving the diversity of the VLM data. We will release all code, data and models for future research.

Not Just a Subject: Capturing Adaptive Scene-Specific Variance for Visual Storytelling
Authors: SeungJu Cha, Ye-Chan Kim, Kwanyoung Lee, Dong-Jin Kim
First: 2026-10-15T17:51:49+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:51:49+00:00
Comments: Accepted at ACCV 2026. Code: https://github.com/SeungJuCha/EOS-Story
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Recent advancements in diffusion models have significantly improved text-to-image generation, enabling their application to visual storytelling, where coherent image sequences are generated from narrative prompts. However, existing methods primarily focus on identity preservation and often fail to adapt to diverse scene variations, resulting in semantic misalignment and structural inflexibility. We propose EOS-Story, a training-free framework that harmonizes identity consistency with scene-specific adaptation across text and vision spaces. In the text space, we decouple the narrative into identity and scene sub-prompts to prevent information dilution, but this leaves the scene without access to appearance cues. To bridge this gap, we introduce Cross-Contextual Bridging, which softly injects a phrase-level appearance residual into the scene's <EOS> embedding. In the vision space, we find that scale mismatch between identity and scene subjects causes a few identity tokens to be repeatedly transferred, producing structural artifacts. We address this with Adaptive Identity Preservation, which anchors identity features at the scene-specific scale (Identity Anchoring) and integrates them via Identity-Aware Self-Attention. To evaluate performance under varied spatial compositions, we extend the Consistory+ benchmark with diverse framing scenarios (Consistory+_+). Extensive experiments show that EOS-Story achieves competitive or superior performance on both benchmarks.

Velox: Spatial Memory Makes World Models Efficient
Authors: Qiuhong Shen, Shizun Wang, Xinchao Wang
First: 2026-10-15T17:50:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:50:20+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Video world models turn actions and camera motion into long visual rollouts, making video a natural interface for interactive simulation. Such interaction requires responsive generation, yet coherent rollouts must retain visual memory of what has already been generated. Chunked video diffusion models usually keep this context in growing KV caches, so later chunks become progressively slower. To address this bottleneck, we introduce Velox, a training-free attention router for region-level context selection using frustum slab correspondences from streaming geometry. Velox represents historical blocks as camera frustum slabs with depth intervals and obtains current-block depth through recency-priority multiwarp. For well-covered regions, frustum slab overlap softly modulates each layer's model-derived block importance. Uncovered regions retain the model-derived route without geometric bias. The fixed token-block granularity matches FlashAttention-style tiled execution and reduces the attention term that grows with rollout length. Frustum slab construction runs asynchronously with denoising on the same H200. At chunk 48, Velox accelerates attention by $4.36\times$ and denoising by $2.43\times$. On the full 158-case WBench navigation split, it remains within 0.15 API-free average points of dense LingBot-Fast-Cam. Please see our project page at https://velox-world.com/velox/ for more details.

LEASH: Uncertainty-Aware Weak-to-Strong Escalation and Oversight for Computer-Use Agents
Authors: Qingni Wang, Changdae Oh, Yuzhe Yang, Sharon Li, Jiachen Yang, Ang Li, Xin Eric Wang
First: 2026-10-15T17:40:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:40:12+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Computer-use agents increasingly tackle long-horizon tasks, where running a frontier model at every step is expensive and a small open-weight model alone often fails. Weak-to-strong escalation offers a middle ground where a weak agent acts by default and hands control to a strong one only when needed. The core challenge is deciding when to escalate, which requires an uncertainty signal cheap enough to check at every step. However, even after handoff, the strong agent can still get lost, but a black-box API that the strong agent is usually served exposes no internals to monitor. To this end, we propose LEASH, a training-free framework that unleashes the strong agent only when the weak agent's latent uncertainty signals it is needed, and keeps it on a leash afterward. LEASH has three layers: (1) initial task uncertainty for early routing, (2) step-level uncertainty for runtime escalation, and, after handoff, (3) a shadow observer for continued oversight of the strong black-box agent. Here, we use latent uncertainty as a fuel for all three layers. Specifically, we compare the weak model's hidden states with those from its past successful and failed trajectories, and a state that looks more like the failures signals higher uncertainty. During weak-agent execution, our uncertainty estimator reuses activations already produced during action generation, so it needs no extra forward pass or dedicated monitor training. Experiments on OSWorld across four contemporary model backbones show that LEASH consistently improves over standalone weak agents and outperforms random escalation by 6.55--11.94 points at matched escalation rates. With Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, LEASH achieves 73.66% task success at $0.4117 per task, matching always-on Claude Opus 5 (72.90%) while reducing inference cost by 19.2%.

MJev-Spatial: System One Spatial Intelligence via Native Attention Matching
Authors: Luozheng Qin, Yuhan Zhu, Changhai Zhou, Qian Qiao, Jun Gao, Yuhua Zhou, Harry Zhang, Xiangyu Zeng, Yuxuan Liu, Kaijie Chen, Andrew Chen, Pony Ma
First: 2026-10-15T17:37:50+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:37:50+00:00
Comments: 37 pages, 7 figures, 27 tables. Code: https://github.com/Fr0zenCrane/MJev-Spatial
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Spatial intelligence lets an agent perceive and act in the physical world, and many of its judgments must be made at interaction rates: which spatial relation holds, how far an object is, or where to place it. Vision-language models (VLMs) produce these answers by free-form text generation, paying for autoregressive decoding and answer parsing even though the output space is compact: one of a few options, a scalar, or a 2D point. In light of this, we introduce MJev-Spatial, a System 1 multimodal decision model that tackles all three types of spatial understanding tasks within a unified classification framework. Specifically, MJev-Spatial treats each candidate as a class and scores it using the backbone's pretrained query and key projections, without adding parameters. The candidates are the given options for choice tasks, or value bins and image regions refined through coarse-to-fine classification for numerical and point prediction, respectively. At inference, each classification decision takes a single forward pass and generates no answer tokens. To train on heterogeneous spatial data efficiently, we supervise all coarse-to-fine stages jointly with teacher forcing in one forward pass, and combine this with length-aware global batching and cross-GPU load balancing, raising training throughput by 30.8% over the original batching strategy. Built on Qwen3.5-4B and trained on 12.3M question-answer pairs converted from existing spatial datasets, MJev-Spatial achieves the highest overall score (65.64%) among eight tested models, ranks first on seven of twelve benchmarks, and outperforms its unmodified autoregressive base model on all twelve, while running 2.5 times faster than the fastest autoregressive baseline and 5.6 times faster than the base model.

Training-Free On-Policy Distillation
Authors: Bangji Yang, Jiajun Fan, Zhaoyu Zhu, Hongbo Ma, Ge Liu
First: 2026-10-15T17:15:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:15:01+00:00
Comments: 20 pages, 6 figures, 9 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
On-policy distillation consolidates specialist models into one student at the price of rollouts, teacher passes and gradient steps for every teacher. We show that the consolidation does not need training: with forward passes only, the capabilities of separately tuned specialists can be aggregated into one model that is, on average, more accurate than either specialist alone. When specialists share a base model, distillation has an exact layer-wise solution: each linear layer of the fused model must reproduce every specialist's output on the inputs that specialist receives while reading its own answers, a least-squares problem whose solution needs only the second moments of those inputs. One forward pass over a few hundred rollouts provides them. We call this training-free on-policy distillation (\method). What the rollouts contain matters more than the solver: answers truncated in repetition loops are few among a mathematics specialist's rollouts but carry a large share of their tokens, and removing them lifts the fused model on AIME25 from task arithmetic's level to the specialist's at matched precision. On Olmo~3 7B, a few GPU-hours of forward computation per specialist yield a generalist that keeps most of each specialist's gain on each of seven mathematics and code benchmarks.

On-policy distillation consolidates specialist models into one student at the price of rollouts, teacher passes and gradient steps for every teacher.
Skill-Decoupled On-Policy Expert Distillation for Unified Vision-Language Models
Authors: Yuqi Xiong, Chunyi Peng, Zhipeng Xu, Qing Yang, Zhenghao Liu, Zulong Chen, Yu Gu, Ge Yu
First: 2026-10-15T17:01:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:01:41+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Reinforcement learning (RL) can elicit specialized visual capabilities in vision-language models (VLMs), yet jointly optimizing heterogeneous objectives often introduces cross-capability gradient conflicts. Training experts independently mitigates this interference, but direct reward optimization may entangle specialized knowledge with reward-hacking biases. This paper proposes Skill-Decoupled On-Policy Expert Distillation (SCOPED), which trains capability-specific experts and extracts explicit skills through reward-guided reflection. During on-policy distillation, these skills guide expert supervision while remaining unavailable to the unified student, encouraging it to internalize procedural knowledge without explicit skills at inference time. Across eight multimodal benchmarks, SCOPED consistently outperforms the backbone and mixed-capability RL, demonstrating the effectiveness of skill-decoupled on-policy expert distillation for integrating independently acquired capabilities. Further analyses reveal interference during joint capability optimization and suggest that skill augmentation promotes more consistent expert trajectories, greater attention to task-relevant visual evidence, and better preservation of specialized expertise in the unified student. All code and data are available at https://github.com/NEUIR/SCOPED.

LAMP: Layerwise Adaptive Mixing of Predictors for Training-Free Few-Shot Vision-Language Adaptation
Authors: Zhipeng Ye, Feng Jiang, Qiufeng Wang, Hao Li
First: 2026-10-15T16:35:40+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T16:35:40+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Few-shot adaptation often relies on an encoder's final global representation, leaving complementary information in intermediate layers underused. Combining these features is challenging because their dimensions can differ and their usefulness varies across tasks. We propose LAMP, Layerwise Adaptive Mixing of Predictors, a training-free framework that combines global and regional information from multiple encoder layers. A single forward pass per view supplies all descriptors. Global and support-calibrated regional kernel predictors independently fit the same label residuals. Labeled validation data select their mixture in class-score space, preserving native feature dimensions without learned projections. LAMP-F further adapts text-anchored class prototypes, using a cached response to update the fixed-kernel residual predictions exactly. Experiments cover eleven datasets and ViT and ResNet backbones. LAMP achieves state-of-the-art mean accuracy among the compared published training-free ResNet-50 methods at 4, 8, and 16 shots, reaching 78.73% at 16 shots. Averaged across five support budgets, LAMP improves over a paired global-feature-only baseline by 1.14 and 1.59 percentage points on ViT-B/16 and ResNet-50; LAMP-F raises these gains to 1.96 and 2.54 points. LAMP-F reaches 16-shot accuracies of 83.57% and 79.10%, respectively. Controlled experiments identify multi-layer integration as the main source of improvement and examine pooling, calibration, validation labels, and computational cost. These findings show that combining layer-specific predictions can exploit complementary features from frozen encoders to improve few-shot classification.

Abstract
We introduce REKI, a 2.4B vision-language-action model (VLA) pretrained exclusively on public data that sets a new Pareto frontier between performance and model size: on LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin Clean2Rand and RoboCasa365, it is outperformed only by models about twice its size. REKI consists of a vision-language model (VLM) followed by a flow-matching action expert, and is pretrained on a curated mixture of public robot, egocentric human and vision-language data. Additionally, we endow it with a memory that gives the policy access to its recent past at negligible cost: a few register tokens in the action expert summarise the output of the VLM at each frame and persist in the expert's cache. This memory improves success in simulation and on real robots, and makes oscillations between poses about four times rarer. REKI uses a simple model design and training paradigm, and is optimised to be efficient at training and inference. We release the full code across data curation, training, inference and evaluation, deployment, and all pretrained and finetuned model weights: https://github.com/kyutai-labs/reki.

EMO: A Closed-Loop Test-Time Computation Framework via Alternating Context Optimization and Logits Steering
Authors: Wei Xia, Yehui Tang, Zhi-Hong Deng
First: 2026-10-15T16:23:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T16:23:55+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Test-time context optimization adapts frozen language models through textual feedback, but ordinary conditional generation provides no separate control over how strongly to reinforce the distributional changes induced by a context update. We introduce EMO, a training-free framework inspired by expectation--maximization that alternates question-specific context estimation (E-step) and distribution refinement (M-step). The E-step revises a system context using textual gradients from an external judge without access to gold answers. The M-step contrasts next-token distributions under the revised and preceding contexts at a shared prefix to construct a confidence-constrained logit correction. Feedback on the steered response drives the next context update, coupling adaptation across rounds with intervention within each generation. Theoretically, we connect parameter and context optimization through a shared target-KL objective and additive logit representations, and derive the steering vector from context-induced logit displacements. Under context-improvement and faithful-steering assumptions, we prove a one-round decrease in target KL divergence for sufficiently small steering strength. Under greedy decoding across eight models and six benchmarks, EMO improves the zero-shot baseline in 39 of 40 aggregated settings, averaging $+9.10$ percentage points. With the same judge, it adds $+1.53$ points on average over context-only optimization (TextGrad), with consistent MMLU-Pro gains across models. At matched target-model generation budgets, accuracy advantages over best-of-$N$ are strongest on Llama, while oracle coverage is higher in most comparisons despite fewer candidates. The coverage--selection gap motivates allocating test-time computation jointly to candidate generation and verification.

Abstract
In pathology, pattern recognition relies on a frame of reference built through extensive training and experience, which may still fall short when encountering rare cancer subtypes. Archives of previously diagnosed tumors often contain relevant precedents, yet identifying the most relevant cases remains challenging. Here we explore whether content-based retrieval with current pathology foundation models facilitates rare cancer diagnostics without any task-specific training. Abstract
Recent work shows that depth recurrence can be retrofitted into pretrained language models. In this work, we adapt this paradigm for visual understanding, using depth recurrence to reduce the parameter footprint of a pretrained vision-language model (VLM). To this end, we prune layers from the language backbone of the VLM and loop over a subset of the remaining layers. At each step of the loop, a linear adapter merges the block's output with the token representations from the preceding layers and projects the fused representation back into the block.
