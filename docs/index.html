Privacy Accounting for Empirical Denoisers
Authors: William Cheng, Dongjun Kim, Mijung Park
First: 2026-10-20T06:30:08+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T16:49:44+00:00
Comments: 31 pages, 7 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Diffusion models have emerged as the foundation of modern generative systems, yet their high memorization capacity raises privacy concerns. While differentially private (DP) training provides formal guarantees, it comes at a cost in sample quality. In this work, we study a complementary question: how much privacy loss is incurred during the sampling process itself? Directly analyzing modern neural denoisers is challenging because their sensitivity to a single training example is generally intractable. To overcome this challenge, we construct and analyze a clipped privacy-accountable empirical denoiser, a training-free estimator of the Bayes-optimal denoiser, whose per-step sensitivity can be computed explicitly, allowing each denoising step to be a Gaussian mechanism. This perspective enables privacy accounting under Gaussian DP when each training example contributes to each denoising step, and under $f$-DP trade-off functions when each generated sample uses a single randomly subsampled block of the training data. We report every $(\varepsilon, δ)$ guarantee as a certified upper bound from a privacy loss distribution accountant, and use a cumulant-based Edgeworth approximation as a fast diagnostic, characterizing where it is reliable and where it becomes anti-conservative. We also analyze the privacy loss along the denoising trajectory and show that it grows steeply near the critical window in which semantic content is formed. This observation motivates hybrid samplers that use the private empirical denoiser through the formative steps where it remains a faithful proxy for the neural denoiser, then switch to a public neural denoiser for the remaining refinement. Our results on CIFAR-10 and CelebA-64 show that the privacy of diffusion models depends not only on the training procedure, but also on sampling-time design choices such as subsampling, stopping time, and hybrid denoising.

ClunoGS: Compact Language 3D Gaussian Splatting via Unified Optimization
Authors: Jian Xu, Yi Li, Ying Huang, Siyi Wu, Bingzhe Li, Yuede Ji, Miao Yin
First: 2026-10-21T16:43:53+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T16:43:53+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Language 3D Gaussian Splatting enables open-vocabulary scene understanding by associating Gaussian primitives with language features. These semantic attributes increase model storage, while most existing 3DGS compression methods are designed mainly to preserve RGB rendering quality. One straightforward approach is to apply sparsification and quantization sequentially. However, pruning based only on rendering quality can remove Gaussians that have small RGB contributions but are important for semantic prediction. Once removed, their semantic information cannot be recovered by later quantization. We propose ClunoGS, a compact framework for Language 3DGS that combines semantics-aware Gaussian sparsification and vector quantization in a unified constrained objective. Semantic supervision is used during sparsification, and the objective jointly considers rendering quality, semantic accuracy, and compactness. We optimize the unified objective with alternating primal, sparsification, quantization, and dual updates. We evaluate ClunoGS on open-vocabulary localization and segmentation using the LERF and ScanNet-10 benchmarks. Under aggressive compression, our unified, semantics-aware formulation substantially alleviates the degradation of semantic representations caused by sequential compression and semantics-agnostic Gaussian sparsification. Ablations further show that jointly coordinating sparsification and quantization better preserves semantic accuracy than applying them independently.

Training-free Acceleration for Blind Compressed Image Restoration via Activation-Calibrated Singular Value Decomposition
Authors: Suhang Yao, Xin Li, Xijun Wang, Yufei Wang, Yeying Jin, Zhibo Chen
First: 2026-10-21T16:16:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T16:16:41+00:00
Comments: 12 pages. Accepted for publication in IEEE Journal on Emerging and Selected Topics in Circuits and Systems
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Transformer-based models achieve strong performance on blind compressed image restoration (CIR) under unknown compression settings, but their densely stacked projection operators make deployment costly. We study training-free acceleration for such models through an empirical, operator-level analysis. Specifically, we profile where computation is concentrated, characterize channel-wise activation distributions under compressed inputs, and examine the singular-value decay of representative projection weights. These measurements identify the recurring projections in the attention, feed-forward, convolutional, and prompt-conditioning pathways as effective low-rank replacement targets that preserve the original inference graph. They further establish truncated SVD (TSVD) as a principled training-free baseline for blind CIR and reveal why a uniform low-rank objective is mismatched to the channel-imbalanced feature distributions of compressed images. Accordingly, we formulate Activation-Calibrated Singular Value Decomposition (ASVD) for this restoration setting, deriving low-rank factors entirely offline from pretrained weights and lightweight activation statistics gathered from only a few compressed calibration images. By weighting the decomposition with channel-wise activation magnitudes, our ASVD assigns larger penalties to errors on more active channels, requiring neither retraining nor online calibration and leaving the restoration pipeline intact. When instantiated on PromptCIR at rank 64, ASVD consistently improves PSNR-B over TSVD across four standard benchmarks under identical parameter and MAC budgets. At this operating point, the factorized model cuts parameters by 59% (34.75M to 14.34M) and computation by 16.1% (258.79 to 217.16 GMACs for a 256 x 256 input), enabling acceleration without retraining.

Abstract
Automated radiology report generation for multi-sequence brain MRI holds strong promise for alleviating radiologists' workload, yet it remains a demanding problem: brain MRI studies are inherently volumetric, each pulse sequence carries complementary diagnostic information, and annotated data are critically scarce for low-resource clinical languages. Existing brain MRI-report resources are still dominated by English reports, translated clinical text, or datasets adapted from task-specific imaging benchmarks, leaving routine low-resource clinical reporting and heterogeneous multi-sequence MRI protocols insufficiently represented. To bridge this gap, we introduce ViBMR, to the best of our knowledge, the first large-scale multi-sequence brain MRI dataset paired with routine Vietnamese clinical reports, comprising 3,649 patients and 11,875 MRI series across T1, T2, FLAIR, and contrast-enhanced T1 sequences. Building on ViBMR, we establish a comprehensive evaluation setup for brain MRI report generation, covering representative medical vision-language models, multi-sequence inputs, and feature aggregation strategies. The results provide key insights into dataset characteristics and model behavior, while demonstrating the necessity of native low-resource clinical datasets for reliable Vietnamese-origin brain MRI report generation. InfoQuant: Shaping Activation Distributions for Low-Bit LLM Quantization
Authors: Ke Li, Dong An, Xiaoling Zang, Can Ye, Liang Xie, Qibo Qiu, Chen Shen, Xiaofei He, Wenxiao Wang
First: 2026-05-25T05:34:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T14:39:26+00:00
Comments: EMNLP2026 main
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Low-bit activation quantization remains a major bottleneck in efficient large language model (LLM) deployment. The difficulty is not only that activations contain outliers, but that their distributions are often poorly matched to a low-bit uniform quantizer. Existing post-training quantization (PTQ) methods suppress peaks, balance channels, or minimize reconstruction error, yet they rarely specify what activation distribution is actually easy to discretize. As a result, activations may appear numerically smoother while still incurring large quantization error because the quantization range remains wide or most values collapse into a few levels near the mean. We recast activation transformation as quantizer-facing distribution design and analyze quantization error from an information-theoretic perspective. Our analysis shows that quantization-friendly activations should jointly have a smaller numerical range and sufficient dispersion within that range. Guided by this analysis, we propose InfoQuant, a train-free method that employs Peak Suppression Orthogonal Transformation (PSOT) to shape activations into more quantization-friendly distributions. We further introduce adaptive outlier-token selection to improve the robustness of PSOT during optimization. Across multiple LLM families, InfoQuant consistently outperforms prior PTQ and end-to-end training baselines. Under W4A4KV4, it preserves 97% of floating-point accuracy on average and reduces the LLaMA-2 13B performance gap by 42% over the previous state of the art. Code is available at [https://github.com/LLIKKE/InfoQuant](https://github.com/LLIKKE/InfoQuant)

Abstract
Compositional zero-shot learning (CZSL) aims to recognize novel combinations of previously observed objects and attributes. Prior work showed that large-scale vision-language models encode decomposable semantic structures that can be recomposed to enable generalization. The Geodesically Decomposable Embeddings (GDE) framework formalizes this by identifying directions in the tangent space at the Frechet mean of the embedding distribution, which encode primitive visual concepts. Unseen attribute-object compositions can be synthesized through vector addition of these primitives. We revisit the assumption that compositional generalization requires explicit attribute-object pair re-composition. By means of new compositional scoring functions and extensive evaluations, we show that directly using the decomposed primitives suffices for recognizing unseen compositions. In that, we gain insights on how the geometry of the embedding manifold (Euclidean, spherical, or hyperbolic) and dataset structure affect compositional reasoning. Across models (CLIP, DINOv2, MERU, HyCoCLIP), we show that compositional structure consistently emerges as primitive representations, enabling inference on CZSL benchmarks (MIT-States, VAW-CZSL, UT-Zappos50K, Clothing16K) and group robustness on Waterbirds and CelebA. Open-Vocabulary Models and Label Propagation for Uncertainty-aware Pseudo-Labeling of Object Detection Datasets
Authors: Jonathan Klees, Timo Heller, Matthias Rottmann
First: 2026-10-21T14:15:35+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T14:15:35+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Object detection models rely on large-scale annotated datasets that require substantial manual annotation effort. The labeling costs further increase when annotation uncertainty is captured through soft labels, as multiple annotations must be collected for each object. We address this challenge by introducing a semi-supervised framework for the uncertainty-aware annotation of object detection datasets. Our approach leverages open-vocabulary object detectors to generate region proposals and semantically meaningful object representations. Based on these representations, we construct an object-level similarity graph and propagate probabilistic label information. Our framework assigns soft labels to previously unlabeled objects, quantifying both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty. We evaluate our method on four object detection benchmarks with soft-label annotations and demonstrate that high-quality annotations can be obtained with only a small fraction of manually labeled objects. Furthermore, we show that detectors trained on semi-automatically annotated data achieve performance comparable to models trained on fully manually annotated data. We achieve state-of-the-art downstream detection performance for the task of semi-supervised object detection on the COCO dataset, with 40.5% AP virtually matching the performance of the model trained on fully manually constructed data (40.9%) at only 1% of labeling costs. These results highlight the potential of open-vocabulary models and graph-based label propagation for efficient, accurate and uncertainty-aware pseudo-labeling.

SPARE: Sparsity Allocation via Routing Entropy for Efficient MoEs
Authors: Yifan Yang, Hanling Yi, Dongquan Yang, Feng Lin, Rong Xiao, Aijun Li, Xiaotian Yu
First: 2026-10-21T12:40:00+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T12:40:00+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures scale model capacity efficiently, yet their substantial memory footprint poses a major barrier to deployment. Expert pruning reduces this footprint, but most methods focus on which experts to remove and prune every layer equally. We propose SPARE (Sparsity Allocation via Routing Entropy), a training-free, selector-agnostic method that decides how many experts to remove from each layer using routing entropy, the entropy of tokens' normalized Top-K gate weights on calibration data. When a token's gate weight is spread evenly, removing one active expert discards little of it, so SPARE prunes layers with more diffuse routing more aggressively. A perturbation bound under random pruning formalizes this intuition, and controlled interventions with a fixed selector link concentrated routing to larger output changes. With the same selector and budget, SPARE improves average accuracy over uniform allocation on Mixtral, Qwen3, and DeepSeek models, especially at high pruning ratios. At 50% expert pruning, it raises the eight-task average of Qwen3-30B-A3B from 57.2% to 63.8%, and the math, code, and science average of DeepSeek-R1 from 80.1 to 82.8.

Abstract
In remote sensing, open-vocabulary segmentation is particularly important for flexible and scalable land-cover understanding across diverse regions, as it improves generalization to novel categories without requiring exhaustive pixel-level annotations. Despite its great promise, progress in this area remains largely constrained by existing datasets with limited category coverage and overly coarse taxonomies, and therefore struggles to support fine-grained semantic understanding required in real-world applications such as urban functional zoning, carbon accounting, and post-disaster assessment. To address these challenges, we present OpenEarth-OVS44, a multimodal benchmark with 290,587 expert-verified segments from 2,832 RGB images and 1,341 strictly registered RGB-SAR pairs at sub-meter spatial resolution, covering 40 cities across France, Japan, and the United States under a unified 44-class taxonomy organized into 9 base categories. Three evaluation tracks are provided: an RGB-only fine-grained benchmark, a multimodal track with diverse synthetic cloudy conditions for weather-robust evaluation, and a held-out cross-region track. Extensive experiments on representative open-vocabulary segmentation models demonstrate that training on OpenEarth-OVS44 substantially improves fine-grained segmentation performance over the strongest training-free baseline, with improvements of up to 7.1 mIoU points on unseen categories and 27.6 mIoU points on seen categories. The dataset and code will be released.

Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) achieve remarkable performance on 2D medical images, such as X-rays or pathology images, while 3D VLMs that directly process CT/MRI volumes lag behind due to limited 3D training data and the predominance of 2D foundation architectures. To leverage powerful 2D VLMs for analyzing medical volumes, which contain hundreds of slices, it is therefore necessary to focus only on those few slices, which are diagnostically relevant. To enable such a prioritization, we propose XGLASS, a method that transfers medical expertise from 2D X-rays to 3D CT for task-aware slice prioritization. XGLASS uses disease-conditioned importance maps derived from language-aligned radiologist gaze on X-rays and spatially aligns them with coronal CT projections, bringing gaze priors and CT content into a shared 2D representation. The fused gaze-prior/CT projection is then used to predict a disease-specific ordering of axial slices, prioritizing diagnostically relevant slices. XGLASS significantly improves budgeted retrieval over 2D and 3D-native baselines, achieving a top-10 hit rate up to 75.9% for the best-performing disease. In practice, this means that after inspecting only the top 10 slices (4.17% of a 240-slice scan), XGLASS identifies a diagnostically relevant slice in more than 75% of cases. For downstream slice-wise finding classification, an XGLASS-selected 30-slice candidate set reduces model calls by 87.5% while improving F1 by 11.3 percentage points over all-slice inference, 4.2 points over the strongest 2D selector, and remains competitive with 3D-native selectors while requiring up to 130.8 times less time and 10.9 times less GPU memory per finding, supporting its use as a lightweight preselector for volumetric CT analysis. The code and pretrained checkpoint are available on https://github.com/michaelgloeckler/XGLASS.

NormLift: From Lifted Features To Semantic Reliability In 3D Gaussian Splatting
Authors: Yihan Zang, Da Li, Dominik Engel, Shinkyu Park, Ivan Viola

Abstract
Training-free weighted aggregation is widely used to lift 2D semantic features onto 3D Gaussians for open-vocabulary scene understanding, yet its theoretical role remains insufficiently understood. Existing analyses typically justify this operation from the rendering side, treating Gaussian features as linearly composable Euclidean variables for reconstructing 2D feature maps. However, this view does not match downstream 3D usage, where each Gaussian is often queried independently in a cosine-based embedding space. We revisit feature lifting from the 3D side and formulate per-Gaussian assignment as a cosine alignment problem on the CLIP unit sphere. Under this objective, the $\ell_2$-normalized semantic back-projected feature emerges as the closed-form solution, providing a complementary interpretation of the standard lifting rule from the perspective of per-Gaussian semantic assignment. This solution is still a convex combination of the observed directions, so every lifted feature is inherently a mixture. Such mixing is unavoidable for any per-Gaussian estimator, and the question is whether it can be measured. The same formulation answers this: the norm of the back-projected feature decomposes into intra-view and inter-view consistency, so the feature magnitude quantifies how mixed the observations are and serves as a semantic reliability signal. Calibrated by effective multi-view support, this reliability score identifies the Gaussians whose lifted direction has drifted and guides a mode-voting refinement that corrects them by copying a single observed CLIP direction instead of averaging. Experiments on open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation show that NormLift is an efficient, training-free framework that achieves strong performance across evaluation protocols. Our code is available at https://github.com/kaust-violab/NormLift_release

Mitigating Query Shortcuts in Chain-of-Thought with Context-Aware Decoding
Authors: Shin'ya Yamaguchi
First: 2026-10-21T10:05:25+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T10:05:25+00:00
Comments: 9 pages, 3 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
While Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting improves the reasoning performance of large language models (LLMs), recent studies have shown that final answers do not always depend on the generated rationales. In particular, LLMs may rely on query-specific shortcuts rather than the information provided by their reasoning steps. In this paper, we investigate whether context-aware decoding (CAD), an existing contrastive decoding method, can mitigate such shortcut behavior when applied to CoT reasoning. We study this application as Rationale Contrastive Decoding (RCD), which contrasts the next-token distributions conditioned on the query with and without its rationale, amplifying tokens whose likelihood increases due to the rationale. We provide a theoretical interpretation of this decoding rule via a KL-regularized reward-maximization objective. Experiments across five reasoning benchmarks demonstrate that RCD improves answer accuracy over standard CoT. Furthermore, intervention analyses using ground-truth and random rationales reveal that RCD increases the behavioral dependence of final answers on the rationale content. Our findings suggest that contrastive decoding offers a simple, training-free approach to mitigating query shortcuts.

What Transfers from Text to Vision? Capability Scaling Laws and Transfer Dynamics for VLMs
Authors: Ziran Li, Qiang Wang, Zhengyu Chen, Shanglin Lei, Borun Chen, Jingang Wang, Xunliang Cai
First: 2026-06-24T13:23:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T09:33:38+00:00

Abstract
Choosing the right large language model (LLM) backbone is the most consequential decision when building a vision-language model (VLM), yet it remains fundamentally unprincipled: compute-based scaling laws fail to generalize across model families, and no framework exists for directly predicting VLM performance before training begins. We propose the Capability-Driven Multimodal Scaling Law, the first cross-family framework that predicts VLM benchmark accuracy from directly observable textual capability. Given a low-dimensional capability score $S$ extracted from LLM textual benchmarks via PCA, we model VLM performance as a function of $S$, with a per-backbone transfer rate and an absorption rate that quantifies data-scaling efficiency. To fit and validate the framework, we train over 150 VLMs on 34 LLMs spanning 7 model families under a strictly controlled recipe. Evaluations on more than 200 textual and 50 multimodal benchmarks show that the law accurately extrapolates transfer rate from models up to 8B parameters to 72B-scale backbones, predicts full VLM training trajectories with high fidelity, and generalizes to entirely held-out model families. Beyond the scaling law, our analysis surfaces actionable insights: certain textual benchmarks negatively correlate with multimodal performance, exposing latent benchmark-gaming behavior; base LLMs outperform instruction-tuned counterparts as VLM backbones due to higher absorption rates and lower data-scaling decay; and different model families occupy distinct positions in the transfer--absorption space. The framework turns backbone selection from costly empirical sweeps into a principled, quantitative decision. Code and data are available at https://github.com/wangq-dev/CDMScaling.

BlazeVGGT: 13x Faster VGGT at 2,000 Frames via Camera Token without Training
Authors: Shujun Yao, Junhui Lin, Congying Han, Tiande Guo, Ziwen Liu, Bonan Li

Abstract
Feed-forward visual geometry models, exemplified by VGGT, have demonstrated strong multi-view reconstruction capabilities through dense global attention. However, the resulting all-to-all cross-view computation scales quadratically with the number of input views, making long-sequence inference particularly expensive due to substantial frame-level redundancy among highly overlapping observations. We uncover a topology-first, geometry-later evolution in VGGT camera tokens: shallow-layer tokens reliably capture local view neighborhoods, whereas deeper-layer tokens progressively encode globally organized inter-view geometry. Motivated by this observation, we introduce BlazeVGGT, a training-free framework for structure-aware frame-level key--value sparsification. At each global-attention block, BlazeVGGT constructs a view-level similarity kernel from camera-token relations and employs greedy determinantal point process inference to select a compact set of mutually complementary views. The selected views constitute the key--value context, while all input frames are retained as queries. This asymmetric attention design preserves dense per-frame predictions while eliminating redundant cross-view computation, allowing our method to be directly integrated into pretrained VGGT as a plug-and-play module without parameter updates. Its advantages become increasingly pronounced with sequence length: at 2,000 frames, BlazeVGGT achieves approximately 13$\times$ faster inference than VGGT while reducing the Chamfer Distance from 0.41 to 0.32. Extensive experiments demonstrate a favorable accuracy--efficiency trade-off in point-cloud reconstruction and camera-pose estimation, compatibility with existing VGGT accelerators such as FastVGGT and LiteVGGT, and transferability to VGGT-based models including StreamVGGT and PAGE-4D. Codes are publicly available at https://github.com/MagiciannnY/BlazeVGGT.git.

Abstract

Sparsity Should Be ALLOTted by Output: Training-Free Allocation of Block-Sparse Attention for Video Diffusion Transformers
Authors: Tianze Xia, Xiaoling Zhou, Ruya Lan, Yiqiao Liao, Chengru Song, Shiming Xiang

Abstract
Full 3D attention is costly in video Diffusion Transformers. Block-sparse attention reduces this cost by computing only selected attention blocks. However, existing methods either overlook the potential to optimize sparsity allocation or rely on heuristic rules or local metrics to guide it. In this work, we observe that sparsifying attention in different layers and at different denoising steps affects final-video fidelity to substantially different degrees. Yet the identities of the most sensitive layer-step pairs remain nearly unchanged across inputs. Building on these findings, we propose Allocating Layer-step Latency by Output Tests (ALLOT), a training-free sparsity allocation method. Treating each layer-step pair as a cell, ALLOT estimates each cell's contribution to final-video error through (i) cell probing and (ii) trajectory calibration, then jointly optimizes cell densities under a measured-latency budget. It preserves existing selection strategies and kernels, replacing only the allocation component. At equal or shorter wall-clock time, ALLOT outperforms every compared allocation strategy with XAttention, with an 11.8 dB PSNR gain on HunyuanVideo. Extensive experiments across four additional published pipelines on Wan2.1, Wan2.2, and HunyuanVideo show that ALLOT outperforms or ties the default in all nine settings.

Abstract
Deploying LLMs for financial information retrieval demands accuracy, cost efficiency, and reproducibility, yet most approaches require expensive fine-tuning.
