DoubtLess: Training-Free Contrastive Decoding for Efficient Reasoning
Authors: Minghao Luo, Liang Chen
First: 2026-10-13T17:59:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:59:32+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Reasoning models achieve strong performance through long chains of thought (CoT), but often spend more tokens than needed to solve a problem. Existing ways to shorten the CoT require additional training for each model, add a fixed steering vector to the model's hidden states, or use decoding heuristics such as stopping early or banning words like "wait", which can reduce accuracy. We propose DoubtLess, a training-free decoding method in which a much smaller reasoning model steers the large one towards brevity. The small model reads the large model's CoT so far twice, once prompted to be brief (the brief thinker) and once prompted to be verbose (the verbose thinker), and the difference between its two logit vectors, scaled by a strength, is added to the large model's logits at every step of the CoT. On seven benchmarks of competition mathematics, graduate-level science and competitive programming, DoubtLess shortens the CoT of Qwen3-32B by 31% with unchanged accuracy, outperforming five inference-time baselines. We also show that DoubtLess reduces thinking tokens per turn by 20% in a self-debugging coding agent with unchanged task performance. It also generalizes to two other model families with unchanged accuracy. When combined with training-based compression, it further shortens the CoT of a model already trained for brevity. Ablations and behavioral analysis show that DoubtLess reduces repeated checking, with most token savings coming after the correct answer first appears in the CoT. Our code is available at https://github.com/leoluolol/DoubtLess.

Visual Tokens on Demand: Visual Budgeting for Scalable and Efficient Multi-Image Reasoning in Multimodal LLMs
Authors: Aimon Rahman, Joymallya Chakraborty, Vidit Bansal, Abhishek Dan
First: 2026-10-13T17:59:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:59:24+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Multimodal large language models have advanced rapidly, but most remain optimized for single-image inputs. Multi-image reasoning is costly because each image adds many visual tokens, increasing encoding and attention demands. Existing techniques that address the above issue, including visual token pruning, token merging, visual token compression, learned resampling, hierarchical compression, retrieval-augmented visual reasoning, and memory-based architectures, have largely been designed for single-image scenarios, where redundancy is primarily spatial and local. Multi-image reasoning introduces additional challenges: redundancy may occur across images, task-relevant evidence may be sparse or distributed, and aggressive per-image compression can remove details needed for comparison, counting, temporal reasoning, retrieval, or evidence grounding. Moreover, existing token-reduction methods are often either (i) training-free but inference-inefficient, typically relying on accessing attention matrices to get token-importance that may be impractical to access with memory-efficient attention implementations such as FlashAttention, or (ii) training-dependent, requiring the base MLLM to be retrained or adapted to a new compressed-token paradigm. In this work, we study scalable multi-image reasoning and understanding without retraining the base model and without accessing attention matrices at inference time. To this end, we propose mmVB, a lightweight plug-and-play module that assigns query-conditioned per-image token budgets via cross-image context, adding only 1.6M trainable parameters while reducing visual input tokens by 9x. This reduction yields 5x faster time-to-first-token and 7x fewer prefill FLOPs, while maintaining 94.4% of full-resolution accuracy across eight multi-image reasoning benchmarks.

Plan-Bench: Can Existing VLMs Go Well Beyond Perception to Reason and Plan in Embodied Worlds?

Abstract
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have achieved strong performance in visual perception and spatial reasoning, leading to growing interest in using them as high-level planners for embodied AI. However, physical tasks require models to integrate spatial information, reason over temporal states, and generate multi-step plans under various constraints, raising the question of whether current VLMs can reliably support such reasoning and planning independently of execution. We introduce Plan-Bench, a benchmark for evaluating the high-level reasoning and multi-step planning capabilities of VLMs relevant to embodied worlds, while isolating the influence of action execution. It covers three complementary dimensions, spanning 16 subclasses and 699 curated samples. Extensive evaluation of 28 VLMs reveals room for improvement, with even the strongest model achieving only 75.0% accuracy. We further find that model scaling generally improves these capabilities. Ablation studies also indicate larger models show increasing potential as high-level planners. Overall, Plan-Bench provides a lightweight probe for evaluating VLMs' reasoning and planning capabilities relevant to embodied environments and assessing their potential as decision makers for physical tasks. Our benchmark and code are available at https://github.com/MinneFeng/Plan-Bench.

Abstract
Visual reasoning is an important capability for vision-language models (VLMs) to understand visual evidence, spatial structure, constraints, and state changes. Existing evaluations mainly rely on real-world data or procedurally generated tasks. Real-world data preserve natural complexity, while procedural games provide controllable generation and verifiable solutions. However, it remains unclear whether abilities measured in toy games correspond to real-world visual reasoning and whether game examples can support transfer. We introduce Game2Real-Bench, a benchmark that studies these connections across four capability groups, including fine-grained perception, spatial reasoning, constraint reasoning, and sequential planning. We construct 1,000 procedurally generated game samples with executable verification and curate 400 real-world samples aligned by shared reasoning requirements rather than visual similarity. We evaluate 25 representative VLMs and observe strong game-to-real consistency, with Pearson and Spearman correlations of 0.935. We further study one-shot game-to-real transfer on four models. Constraint reasoning improves for several models, while fine-grained perception declines with game demonstrations. We also construct Game2Real-Dataset with nearly 24K verified game samples across 18 tasks and 65 QA families. Our benchmark, dataset, and code are available at https://github.com/QingAnLiu/Game2Real-Bench.

Abstract
With the rapid development of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), evaluating their video understanding capabilities has become increasingly important. Existing video understanding benchmarks cover only part of the evaluation dimensions, limiting systematic diagnosis of omni-modal video understanding. We introduce OmniCompass, a holistic benchmark for omni-modal video grounding and understanding. OmniCompass defines two evaluation tracks: OmniCompass-VL assesses vision-language models on tasks requiring visual evidence, while OmniCompass-AV assesses omni-modal models on tasks requiring joint audio-visual evidence. Each track includes three complementary tasks: temporal grounding (TG), spatial grounding (SG), and question answering (QA), assessing when a referenced target appears, where it is, and what happens. QA covers five perception and three reasoning categories in OmniCompass-VL, and three perception and two reasoning categories in OmniCompass-AV. OmniCompass-VL contains 820 videos and 2,460 instances across domains and three duration types: short, medium, and long. A narrative- and audio-rich subset forms OmniCompass-AV with 240 videos and 720 instances. Each instance is evaluated under two paired settings: text-only and text+image, specifying the same target through text and visual-language guidance. All instances are manually annotated with multi-round quality verification. Evaluations of 50 MLLMs show that open-source models still lag behind proprietary counterparts, particularly in audio-visual understanding. Precise temporal and spatial grounding remains challenging even for leading models, and their predictions still exhibit inconsistencies across the paired target specifications.

BLADE: Auditing Information Use for Failure Attribution in LLM Multi-Agent Systems
Authors: Xiaoguang Guo, Yaohao Long, Chuxu Zhang
First: 2026-10-13T17:34:34+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:34:34+00:00
Comments: 16 pages, 6 figures, 3 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Large language model multi-agent systems extend individual agents' capabilities through collaboration across specialized roles, tools, and workflows. Yet collaboration makes failures harder to diagnose: one agent may misinterpret a correct result while others carry the mistake forward. Failure attribution therefore seeks to identify the responsible agent and decisive error step. Recent methods trace dependencies, but knowing where information came from is not the same as knowing where it went wrong. We introduce BLADE (Blame Localization via Audited Dependency Evidence), a training-free framework that moves beyond tracing information to auditing its use. Using Jev for structured probabilistic judgments, BLADE uses dependency probabilities to select relevant source-decision handoffs for auditing. At these handoffs, it examines whether receiving decisions use their sources faithfully and within task requirements, distinguishing error introduction, propagation, and repair. These findings inform a global diagnosis that considers all execution steps. Without reference answers, BLADE achieves the best or tied-best step accuracy among eight strategies under a shared Jev decision interface on 1,228 failed runs across five Who&When and Who&When Pro subsets, outperforming All-at-once by 2.2-11.1 percentage points. Making responsibility evidence explicit offers a path toward more interpretable and reliable agentic systems.

Abstract
We introduce LoopVL to study whether Loop Transformers can be effectively extended to vision- language models. LoopVL combines Module-Loop and Model-Loop computation to iteratively update a unified vision-language state through shared modules. We train LoopVL from scratch through language pre-training, multimodal training, and post-training. LoopVL outperforms a range of similarly sized and larger non-recurrent models on multimodal understanding and visual reasoning benchmarks. We also observe Visual Aha Moments in LoopVL, characterized by pronounced shifts in visual attention across loops. LoopVL provides practical evidence for recurrent vision-language modeling and offers an intuitive perspective on how shared parameters can support deeper multimodal computation over continuously evolving visual-language states.

Abstract
Iterative diffusion generation requires different amounts of model capacity at different token positions. Prior model combinations allocate capacity by denoising step or by drafting and verification role. We present HybridStitch, a training-free framework that uses token-level feedback to select the model supplying each adopted update. Continuous image refinement and discrete language commitment operate on a shared generation state, while model-specific KV buffers provide attention context for selected queries. HybridStitch achieves 1.63--1.68$\times$ faster image generation and 1.15--1.58$\times$ diffusion large language model (dLLM) throughput on four main benchmarks. SP$^2$ec: Adaptive Self-Speculative Decoding for Vision-Language Models
Authors: Yuqi Huang, Xingyao Li, Yunlong Hou, Fengzhuo Zhang, Jiachun Pan, Vincent Y. F. Tan
First: 2026-10-13T17:08:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:08:28+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Speculative Decoding (SD) has demonstrated its efficacy in accelerating large language model inference, but for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), the diverse input modalities make it difficult for a small trained drafter to achieve consistently high throughput. Self-speculative decoding reuses parts of the target model as a drafter which retains the multi-modal capability of the VLMs, yet its efficiency depends on choosing a suitable draft configuration for each prompt. We propose Single-Peak SPeculative decoding (SP$^2$ec), a training-free bandit algorithm that adapts the draft configuration during generation. SP$^2$ec exploits the single-peak throughput pattern observed in existing self-speculative methods, including SWIFT, KnapSpec, SpecVLM, and our simple cosSim. Our algorithmic design explicitly minimizes a novel notion of wall-time regret for each given query, corresponding to the empirical wall-time latency. Theoretical guarantees on the wall-time regret show that SP$^2$ec can exploit the optimal draft configuration, with negligible exploration cost. Extensive experiments on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral-7B across visual and textual tasks show consistent improvements of up to 11\% over the original self-speculative methods, demonstrating the wide applicability of the proposed SP$^2$ec algorithm.

Segment Where You Look: Attention as the Interface Between Language Models and Segmenters
Authors: Eslam Abdelrahman, Hamza Omar Alkaf, Yuheng Li, Siddharth Srinivasan Iyer, Nanxuan Zhao, Eli Shechtman, Krishna Kumar Singh, Mohamed Elhoseiny
First: 2026-10-13T17:06:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:06:29+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) that segment from language must pass the location of the referent to a pixel decoder. The dominant interface compresses it into the hidden state of a single [SEG] token, a d-dimensional vector with no spatial index, and leaves the decoder, which never sees the expression, to recover the referent's extent. We show that the VLM already computes a better signal: the [SEG] token's attention over the image tokens, which is defined on the patch grid by construction. Probing a frozen segmentation VLM, we find that this attention yields 18 to 22 more gIoU than the [SEG] hidden state when read by the same probe, that it gains nothing from the image where the hidden state does, and that it is concentrated in the late layers. Building on these findings, we introduce AttnSeg, which replaces the [SEG] embedding with this attention as the decoder's prompt. A learned, signed combination of the attention heads forms a dense spatial prompt, and an attention-guidance objective supervises it directly. A two-stage recipe first trains the VLM to localize without the decoder, then trains the decoder to refine, within the budget of standard fine-tuning. Applied to Sa2VA, AttnSeg improves ReasonSeg by 6.4 gIoU and GS-Eval by 10.2 at 4B (4.2 and 9.6 over the same model fine-tuned on the same data), and by 8.8 and 11.3 at 14B. The gains reach 13 to 18 gIoU on object parts, multiple objects, and single objects, where the referent must be singled out, and are negligible on stuff regions, which the decoder already recovers unaided. Code and models are available at https://eslambakr.github.io/attseg_project_page/

Unlocking Token Rewards via Training-Free Reward Attribution
Authors: Sitong Wu, Haoru Tan, Bin Xia, Xichen Zhang, Jingyao Li, Shaofeng Zhang, Xiaojuan Qi, Bei Yu, Jiaya Jia
Venue: CVPR 2026
First: 2026-10-13T16:53:27+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T16:53:27+00:00
Comments: token-level reward, credit assignment, PRM, RL
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
In this paper, we propose an extremely efficient, training-free method to extract token-level reward signals directly from an existing deep reward model. Our core idea is to attribute the overall process reward to individual tokens by estimating each token's influence. This influence is defined as the change in the final macroscopic reward (e.g., the process reward) when a token is replaced with a semantically null token. Naively calculating this influence is computationally infeasible, requiring N forward passes through the PRM for an N-token sequence. We overcome this bottleneck by proposing a highly efficient gradient-based estimator. Specifically, we use a first-order Taylor approximation, which simplifies the influence calculation to the inner product of the difference between the token embedding and the null token embedding, and the gradient of the reward with respect to the token embedding. This requires only a single forward and backward pass. The resulting token-level rewards enable standard RL algorithms to perform precise credit assignment without requiring additional reward model training. Experiments on challenging reasoning benchmarks demonstrate that our method substantially improves policy optimization efficiency and enhances the generalization of LLM reasoning capabilities. Our P2T outperforms the outcome reward by +4.9% on MathVista for Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, and +11.5% on AIME24 for Qwen2.5-Math-7B. The code will be released via https://github.com/stonewst/P2T.

Abstract
Multi-fidelity (MF) regression often operates in regimes of extreme data imbalance, where the commonly-used Gaussian-process (GP) surrogates struggle with cubic scaling costs and overfit to sparse high-fidelity observations, limiting efficiency and generalization in real-world applications. We introduce FIRE, a training-free MF framework that couples tabular foundation models (TFMs) to perform zero-shot in-context Bayesian inference via a high-fidelity correction model conditioned on the low-fidelity model's posterior predictive distributions. This cross-fidelity information transfer via distributional summaries captures heteroscedastic errors, enabling robust residual learning without model retraining. Across 31 benchmark problems spanning synthetic and real-world tasks (e.g., DrivAerNet, LCBench), FIRE delivers a stronger performance-time trade-off than seven state-of-the-art GP-based or deep learning MF regression methods, ranking highest in accuracy and uncertainty quantification with runtime advantages. Limitations include context window constraints and dependence on the quality of the pre-trained TFM's.

GeoReader: Learning Latent Geometry for Spatial Reasoning in Vision-Language Models

Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) struggle with metric and multi-view relationships that remain implicit in RGB observations. Geometry foundation models recover this structure from RGB, but existing methods either fuse the estimated features with visual tokens and learn to use them from question answering alone, or align VLM latents with geometry features. Capturing geometry, however, does not ensure its use: zeroing the aligned latents of a released feature-alignment model leaves its accuracy unchanged. We introduce GeoReader, a framework that learns geometric representations for the VLM that reads them. First, we train lightweight Geometric Readers to produce continuous input tokens, supervising them with geometry descriptions decoded by a frozen VLM, so that the Readers express geometry in a form the target model can already read. We then freeze the Readers to preserve this interface while fine-tuning the VLM on spatial QA, with geometry injection conditioned on the question. Geometry descriptions serve only as training targets: at inference, the VLM answers directly from the continuous geometric tokens without generating a geometry chain of thought. With geometry predicted from RGB images, our approach reaches 94.3 on MindCube-Tiny with Qwen3-VL-4B and, with a multi-source QA mixture, an average of 72.5 across six additional spatial benchmarks with Qwen3.5-9B. These results suggest that a useful geometric representation is one the receiving model can use, not merely one from which geometry can be reconstructed.

Knowing the User Is Not Knowing What We Established: Grounding Awareness in Personal Agents
Authors: Jiajun Chen, Xinyue Chen, Yicheng Jiang, Hua Shen
First: 2026-10-13T16:39:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T16:39:59+00:00
Comments: Under Review
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Personal-agent memory has largely focused on learning what users know, prefer, and want. However, knowing the user does not necessarily mean knowing what the user and the agent have established together. An assistant's proposal should not become an authorized instruction merely because it is remembered. We introduce Grounding Awareness, the ability to distinguish accepted, unacknowledged, revised, and scoped arrangements when selecting actions. We develop a controlled benchmark to test whether agents can tell the difference between proposals users have accepted and those they have not, and whether they apply accepted arrangements only where appropriate. Across multiple agents and memory systems, we find that strong memory performance does not ensure Grounding Awareness. More surprisingly, providing more complete user information can increase execution of unacknowledged proposals, while persistent memory can cause agents to treat these proposals as if they were user instructions. We trace these failures through memory writing, retrieval, and action selection. Finally, we introduce Grounding Layer, a lightweight, training-free extension that explicitly tracks grounding status and checks consequential actions, substantially improving agent behavior without modifying models or memory systems.

Abstract
Biomedical language models (LMs) provide strong representations for clinical tasks, but they often fail to capture the hierarchical structure among medical concepts. This limitation is especially pronounced for rare and fine-grained clinical codes in long-tailed vocabularies, where hierarchy-agnostic LM representations become less informative as concept frequency decreases. We introduce Semantic Medical Identifier (SMI), a training-free framework for constructing ontology-grounded Semantic IDs and hierarchy-aware medical concept representations. For each diagnosis, procedure, or medication concept, SMI defines its Semantic ID as the coarse-to-fine path through an expert-defined medical ontology. SMI then aggregates frozen biomedical LM embeddings across the corresponding ontology levels to form hierarchy-aware medical concept representations that preserve coarse-to-fine clinical semantics. The base SMI uses uniform aggregation across ontology levels, while a level-weighted variant selects global level weights on validation data. Our theoretical analysis shows that SMI yields more informative medical concept representations than hierarchy-agnostic LM embeddings, particularly for rare and fine-grained clinical concepts. Experiments show that SMI captures meaningful hierarchical structure and improves clinical relation prediction. On MIMIC-IV, SMI improves mortality prediction, readmission prediction, drug recommendation, and rare drug prediction. On eICU, SMI yields more robust patient representations under cross-hospital distribution shift.

BridgeTwist: Twisting Schrödinger Bridges for Training-Free Conditional Sampling
Authors: Mengyu Li, Qianqian Qu, Jun S. Liu
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-13T16:20:49+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T16:20:49+00:00
Comments: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Generative models are often required to serve many downstream conditional tasks. In the training-free setting, a pretrained unconditional model is reused as a prior and combined with a task-specific likelihood to sample from the corresponding posterior. While such training-free samplers have been developed for diffusion and flow models, their counterpart for Schrödinger bridge (SB) models remains unexplored, despite the growing appeal of SBs as flexible stochastic transport models between general source and target distributions. We propose BridgeTwist, a training-free conditional sampler for pretrained SB generative models, formulated as a tempered twisted sequential Monte Carlo scheme. Its twist is a closed-form plug-in surrogate, obtained by combining the velocity and score fields exposed by SB pretraining into an endpoint predictor and evaluating the likelihood there. Since this surrogate is less reliable near the source, we temper the twist with a time-varying exponent, downweighting its influence at early steps. We further introduce a bridge-shaped inference-time diffusion schedule that concentrates the proposal noise in the interior of the path. Telescoping importance weights then cancel all intermediate twist factors, yielding an asymptotically exact sampler for the posterior. Empirically, BridgeTwist outperforms training-free baselines on class-conditional and text-prompted image generation, and is competitive with specialized inverse problem solvers on inpainting, deblurring, and super-resolution.

Generative models are often required to serve many downstream conditional tasks.
Bayesian Test-Time Inference of Task-Aligned Similarity from Weak Interactive Feedback
Authors: Weida Liang, Kenji Kawaguchi

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Language model systems repeatedly make test-time decisions by comparing similarity: they retrieve exemplars, rank candidates, and revise responses. Yet similarity is not unique: lexical, semantic, structural, and stylistic resemblance can point to different candidates, and the task-aligned notion can change across instances. We introduce BASIS (Bayesian Axis Selection via Interactive Signals), a training-free Bayesian method that uses weak test-time feedback to infer which criterion in a supplied, task-specific library governs the current decision. It maintains a joint posterior over candidate targets and criteria, updates it from scalar scores, pairwise preferences, or verifier outputs, and selects probes by expected information gain. In a finite noisy model, we characterize when the target-criterion pair is recoverable and show why fixed-criterion and fixed-mixture rules can fail under criterion shift.
